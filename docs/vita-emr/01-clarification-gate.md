# Section 15 — Clarification Gate

> Status: **illustrative assumptions**, adopted by explicit direction so the
> rest of this milestone has a concrete foundation to reason about. None of
> these are confirmed business, clinical, or regulatory facts. Anywhere an
> assumption below turns out to be wrong, the "blast radius" note says what
> has to be re-architected, not just re-documented.

| # | Question | Assumption adopted | Blast radius if wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Primary deployment market & regulator; year-2 secondary markets? | **Philippines** — regulator context: Department of Health (DOH) facility licensing, National Privacy Commission (NPC) under the Data Privacy Act (RA 10173), PhilHealth for claims. Year 2: other Southeast Asian markets with similar low-connectivity, mixed-payer profiles (assumption only, not scoped). | Every compliance/localization decision (Section 9, terminology choices, national HIE adapter, payer adapter) is keyed to this. Wrong market ⇒ re-do the compliance map, re-validate the national terminology bindings, and possibly re-architect the HIE adapter's assumed maturity level. |
| 2 | First design partner: clinic, district hospital, or tertiary center? Bed count, provider count, specialty mix? | **A 100-bed district hospital**, general medicine + OB + pediatrics + minor surgery, ~40 providers, plus one **3-provider rural outreach clinic** it supervises — chosen to force both the offline-first and multi-facility requirements from day one. | Wrong partner profile changes the MVP workflow priority order (Section 4 Tier 1) and the Phase 1 pilot success criteria (Section 13). A pure ambulatory clinic partner would let inpatient/BCMA work slip past Phase 3 without anyone noticing it was missing. |
| 3 | Existing systems to integrate/migrate from, and how much historical data? | **None at the design partner** (paper-based today) but assume **HL7 v2 ADT/ORU feeds exist from a regional lab and a regional imaging center** that must be integrated read-only from day one. No bulk historical migration assumed for Phase 1. | If the partner actually runs a legacy EMR, Phase 0/1 must add a data-migration workstream (mapping, dedup against the new MPI, historical note ingestion) that isn't currently budgeted in the roadmap. |
| 4 | Payer landscape: single national payer, private HMOs, cash-pay mix? | **PhilHealth (national) + a handful of private HMOs + significant cash-pay**, concurrently, from day one. | This is why Section 8's payer adapter is designed as "new payer = config, not a sprint" — if the real mix turns out single-payer-only, that generality is over-engineering; if it's more fragmented (10+ HMOs), the adapter needs a partner/aggregator strategy instead of direct integration. |
| 5 | Languages required at launch, including code-switching pairs? | **English + Filipino (Tagalog), with English/Filipino code-switching ("Taglish") as the primary ambient-capture case.** Cebuano is a stated stretch target (per the build prompt's own example) but not committed for launch. | Under-scoping code-switching support degrades Layer 1 (ambient documentation) accuracy specifically for the target market — the one workflow this system is supposed to win on. Over-scoping (committing Cebuano day one) delays Phase 2. |
| 6 | Data residency / sovereignty constraints — can inference leave the country? | **Assume data residency is required for PHI at rest; assume frontier-model inference may cross border only for de-identified or explicitly-consented data, with an in-country/on-prem model tier for anything touching identified PHI.** This is a placeholder, not a legal conclusion. | If residency turns out to be a hard legal requirement with no exception, the entire Layer 1–3 AI stack must run on sovereign or on-prem infrastructure, which changes the model-routing strategy (Section 5) from "cloud frontier for hard cases" to "everything stays at mid-tier or smaller, in-country." |
| 7 | Connectivity reality at the worst site you intend to support? | **Intermittent 3G, single-digit-Mbps, with multi-hour daily outages; grid power itself is unreliable (the build prompt's own "3-bed rural unit with intermittent electricity" scenario is taken as literal, not rhetorical).** | This assumption already drives the offline-first/CRDT-sync ADR (see `adr/0003`). If real sites have better connectivity than this, the offline engineering investment is oversized for Phase 1; if worse (no connectivity for weeks, not days), the 14-day offline target in the build prompt itself needs renegotiating. |
| 8 | Regulatory ambition: SaMD/device clearance for CDS, or administrative/documentation scope only at launch? | **Administrative and documentation scope only at launch. Layer 3 (CDS) ships disabled/shadow-mode only until a device-clearance pathway is deliberately pursued** (see `adr/0010`). | This is the single highest-leverage assumption in this document. It bounds what Section 6's governance spec has to certify for launch, and it means the build order in Section 13 can put CDS in Phase 5 without blocking earlier go-lives. If the business intends to sell CDS as a clinical differentiator at launch, this assumption is wrong and Phase 5 needs to move much earlier, with a real regulatory-pathway workstream funded from Phase 0. |
| 9 | Build, buy, or partner for: DICOM viewer, terminology service, speech recognition, claims clearinghouse? | **Buy/partner for DICOM viewer (zero-footprint OHIF-class viewer) and speech recognition (licensed ASR with medical vocabulary, code-switching fine-tune); build the terminology service in-house** (it's the thing every competitor undervalues, per Section 3); **partner for claims clearinghouse** (payer connectivity is a distribution problem, not a differentiation problem). | Building the terminology service in-house is a real bet — if PH-specific terminology mapping turns out to be thin enough to buy off-the-shelf, that's wasted engineering. Conversely, buying speech recognition assumes a vendor with adequate Filipino/Taglish support exists at acceptable cost; if not, that becomes a build, and Layer 1's 20-second latency budget is at risk. |
| 10 | Team size/composition; embedded practicing clinician? | **Assume yes — one practicing clinician embedded full-time from Phase 0**, with informatics-nursing input from Phase 1 onward. Flagged, per the build prompt's own instruction, as the highest-severity risk if the real answer is no. | Without an embedded clinician, every Tier 1/Tier 2 workflow decision in Section 4 and every alert-budget tuning decision in Section 5/Layer 3 is being made by engineers guessing at clinical workflow — a well-documented failure mode across every EMR post-mortem this benchmark draws on. |
| 11 | Funding runway and the specific commercial milestone that must be hit? | **Assume runway sufficient to reach the end of Phase 3 (inpatient pilot, ~44 weeks per Section 13) before the first renewal/funding decision, with "one district hospital fully live, including inpatient/BCMA, at the stated cost ceiling" as the milestone.** | If the real runway only covers Phase 1 (ambulatory MVP), the roadmap's phase ordering needs to change — Section 13's Phase 4 (Revenue & Agents) has the best near-term ROI-to-effort ratio and may need to move ahead of Phase 3 to protect runway. |
| 12 | Open-source posture: proprietary, open-core, or fully open? | **Open-core**: the FHIR-native platform core, terminology service, and sync engine are assumed open-source (this is also how the "no data hostage-taking" and "acid test" export requirements in Section 8 get credibility); ambient-AI pipeline, agentic-ops layer, and hosted/managed offering are proprietary. | A fully-open posture changes the economics model in Section 12 (no license revenue, services/hosting-only); a fully-proprietary posture undercuts the "open by construction" doctrine (Section 2, #7) and the competitive story against Epic's closed ecosystem. |

## What changes if these assumptions are wrong

The single highest-leverage item on this list is **#8 (regulatory scope)** —
it is upstream of the governance spec (Section 6), the AI feature list that's
allowed to ship user-facing in Phase 1–4, and the roadmap ordering itself.
The second highest-leverage item is **#10 (embedded clinician)** — the build
prompt is explicit that a "no" here is the top project risk, and nothing in
this docs-first milestone can substitute for it. Both should be confirmed
with the actual business/clinical stakeholders before any ADR in this
directory is treated as final rather than draft.
